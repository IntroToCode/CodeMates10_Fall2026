## Proposal: Baw Baw with Dried Fish (Cambodia)

I'd like to propose Cambodian *baw baw* (បបរ, also spelled bobor, borbor or babor) with dried fish. It's plain rice porridge served with crispy fried dried fish, and it's one of the most common breakfasts in Cambodia, from street stalls to home kitchens. Our list doesn't have it yet, and it's simple to make with ingredients that are easy to find.

### Recipe

- **Name:** Baw Baw with Dried Fish (Bâbâr Sâr with Trey Ngeat)
- **Origin:** Cambodia, especially Phnom Penh and the Tonlé Sap region
- **Meal:** Breakfast
- **Difficulty:** Easy
- **Prep time:** 10 min
- **Cook time:** 40 min
- **Total time:** 50 min
- **Servings:** 4

### Historical source

Rice porridge has been an everyday food in Cambodia for a long time, and Wikipedia's entry on Cambodian cuisine describes a typical breakfast as "rice porridge with dried salted fish." The plain, watery style served with salty side dishes comes from Teochew Chinese cooking. Wikipedia's entry on Cambodian Chinese cuisine says Teochew people made up most Chinese migrants to Cambodia until the mid-20th century, and it lists *babor* as a Cambodian Chinese dish. Teochew porridge from Chaozhou is traditionally eaten with salt fish and pickles. The dried fish itself comes from Cambodia's freshwater fisheries. Fish caught when the Tonlé Sap floods each year are salted and sun-dried so they keep through the dry season.

### Ingredients

**Porridge**
- 1 cup jasmine rice
- 8 cups water
- ½ tsp salt

**Dried fish**
- 200 g Cambodian dried fish (*trey ngeat*), or any sun-dried freshwater fish such as snakehead or gourami
- ¼ cup neutral oil for frying

**Toppings and sides**
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced and fried until golden
- 2 scallions, sliced
- 1 thumb ginger, cut into thin matchsticks
- Pickled mustard greens or quick-pickled cucumber
- 2 salted duck eggs, halved (optional)
- Fish sauce, black pepper, fresh chili and lime wedges, to serve

### Instructions

1. Rinse the rice 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear.
2. Bring the water to a boil in a large pot. Add the rice and salt.
3. Lower the heat and simmer uncovered for 30–40 minutes, stirring now and then so it doesn't stick. The grains should be soft and starting to break, and the porridge should be loose. Add hot water if it gets too thick.
4. Meanwhile, cut the dried fish into 2–3 inch pieces. If it's very hard or salty, soak it in warm water for 10 minutes, then pat it completely dry.
5. Heat the oil in a pan over medium heat. Fry the fish for 2–3 minutes per side until it's crisp and golden at the edges, then drain on paper towels.
6. Fry the sliced garlic in the same oil until it's light golden, then remove it right away.
7. Ladle the porridge into bowls and top with fried garlic, scallions, ginger and black pepper.
8. Serve the fried fish, pickles and salted eggs on the side. Each person adds fish sauce, chili and lime to taste and eats a bite of fish with each spoonful.

### Notes

- The porridge is left plain on purpose because the fish and pickles bring the salt and flavor, so taste before adding more seasoning.
- Cambodian, Thai and other Southeast Asian groceries usually carry dried fish. Salted dried mackerel works as a substitute.
